Keep your important papers in order

A simple drawer-and-photos system so you can find your documents when you need them most.

What this coversGeneral orientation. Replacement routes, timelines and fees differ by document and change; always go to the issuing agency’s own page.
Do this first · 2 to 10 minutes

Photograph your ID and one other key document and save them in a folder only you can open.

You do not need to be organised. You need to know where three or four things are, and what you would do if one went missing.

Papers drawer

For each item, tick whether you know where the original is, and whether you have a private photo. Do not write ID numbers anywhere.

ID card(s)

Passport or travel documents, if you have them

Education certificates and results

Enrolment or offer letters

Where your bank account details are (not the numbers)

Vaccination record and list of regular medicines

Insurance policy documents

Hall agreement or tenancy agreement

Employment contract and payslips

Singpass: can you log in, and is there a backup way in?

Ticks are saved on this device only. The photos and scans themselves never come here.

A low-effort system

  1. One physical folder for originals, kept somewhere you can describe to a parent in one sentence.
  2. Photos or scans of each, in a locked or private folder, so you know what you have.
  3. A short note listing what exists and where. Never write passwords or ID numbers in it.
  4. For each document, note which agency issues it. If it is lost, go to that agency’s official page for the replacement process.
ImportantBe careful sharing scans of ID. Black out what is not needed and say what it is for. Do not send ID to people who contacted you first.
What can waitScanning everything you have ever owned. Start with ID, education certificates, and anything you would need for a job or a lease.
What varies, so check yoursWhich documents you hold, and how to replace each, depends on your status. Use the issuing agency’s own guidance.

Sources

This guide makes no official-rule claims, so it cites none. It is general orientation. Where rules matter (your residence, your bank, your employer), their own pages are the source.
