Tax: finding out whether you need to file

How to find out where you stand, and what the main terms mean. We cannot tell you whether you have to file; the myTax Portal can.

What this coversOrientation based on IRAS pages for Year of Assessment 2026 (income earned in 2025), checked 19 Nov 2026. IRAS changes dates and thresholds each year. This is not tax advice.
Do this first · 2 to 10 minutes

Log in to myTax Portal with Singpass and read the message shown for the current Year of Assessment.

Singapore taxes income one year after you earn it. Income earned in 2025 is assessed in Year of Assessment (YA) 2026. Some people get a bill or notice without filing anything, and others must file a return. Which one you are is shown in the myTax Portal, so you need to know how to check.

Step by step: where do I stand?

  1. Log in to myTax Portal with Singpass.
  2. Read the message shown for the current YA. IRAS describes four situations: a Direct Notice of Assessment (you will be told when your bill is ready), the No-Filing Service (you are not required to file), required to file, or no message at all.
  3. If you are required to file, do it by the deadline shown. For YA 2026 IRAS stated 18 April 2026.
  4. If you get no message, you may still need to file. For YA 2026 IRAS’s new-taxpayer page lists, among other conditions, total income in the preceding year of more than $22,000, or self-employment income with a net profit of more than $6,000. Those figures can change, so read the live page.

[1][2]

Year of Assessment (YA)
The year in which tax is assessed on the previous year’s income. [1]
Notice of Assessment (NOA)
The tax bill or statement IRAS sends after assessing your return, or automatically if you are on the No-Filing Service. [2]
No-Filing Service (NFS)
For some people, IRAS assesses tax without a return. You get a notice instead. [2]
myTax Portal
IRAS’s online service for filing and paying, accessed with Singpass. [1]

Evidence from IRAS (checked 19 Nov 2026, YA 2026) [1][2]

  • Filing period for YA 2026: 1 March to 18 April 2026.
  • IRAS says failing to file when required ‘may result in penalties up to $5,000 and/or court summons’.
  • IRAS says to keep proper records of income and expense claims for 5 years (to 31 December 2030 for YA 2026).

MythI am a student, so I do not need to do anything.
Reality: Maybe. Your status depends on your income and situation, and IRAS shows it in the portal. Look rather than assume.
