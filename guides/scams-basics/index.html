Spotting scams, and what to do first

A short, practical starter. The full practice lab is in Scam Shield, a separate section.

What this coversGeneral orientation plus the ScamShield app’s own steps. Scam tactics change quickly; the live official pages are the source of truth. 1799 is the ScamShield helpline as checked earlier from SPF advisories; confirm on the ScamShield site.
Do this first · 2 to 10 minutes

Open ScamShield’s ‘how to check a message’ page and read the four possible outcomes once.

Scams work by getting you to act fast and keep quiet. The best defence is to slow down and tell someone.

The pause rule

  1. Stop. Do not click, reply or pay.
  2. Ask: who contacted me, how did they get my details, what do they want me to do now?
  3. Check through a channel you started yourself, such as the official site or the number on your bank card.
  4. Tell a friend or a parent. Scammers rely on you keeping it secret.
  5. If money or log-in details have gone, call your bank straight away and report it.

Check a message with ScamShield [1]

In the ScamShield app: choose Check for scams, then a message you received, select the source, then upload a screenshot or paste the text and sender. It returns one of four outcomes: looks like a scam, not sure, looks safe, or looks like spam. When it says scam or spam it submits a report for you. [1]

ImportantNever type or paste your Singpass log-in, bank log-in or OTPs into any website you did not open yourself, including this one.
