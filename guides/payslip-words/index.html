Reading your first payslip

What each line means, what a payslip must show, and what to do when something looks wrong.

What this coversOrientation based on MOM’s page on itemised payslips. The sample uses made-up figures. CPF rates depend on your age and status, so check the CPF Board for your own rate.
Do this first · 2 to 10 minutes

Open your latest payslip and tick the 12 items Singapore’s MOM says it should show, if they apply.

A payslip is the receipt for your work. It should tell you who paid you, for which period, what was added, what was taken off, and what you received. Read it every month, especially at the start, because mistakes are easiest to fix early.

What MOM says a payslip should show [1]

Employers must issue itemised payslips to all employees covered by the Employment Act. Items, unless they do not apply to you: employer’s complete name, your complete name, payment date, basic salary (with rate and hours, days or pieces for hourly, daily or piece-rate workers), the start and end of the salary period, allowances, other extra pay such as bonuses, deductions, overtime hours and pay, and total net salary paid. [1]

The payslip should be given with the payment. If that is not possible, it should be given within three working days of payment. Paper or electronic is fine. [1]

If something looks wrong

  1. Compare the net salary on the payslip with what reached your bank account.
  2. Check the dates and any overtime or leave deductions against your own records.
  3. Ask HR or payroll first, in writing, with the line you are asking about. Most errors are admin mistakes.
  4. If it is not fixed and you need official help, start from MOM’s page and look for the right route for your situation.
What varies, so check yoursNot everyone is an ‘employee covered by the Employment Act’. Freelancers and contractors are treated differently. See the freelance guide.
