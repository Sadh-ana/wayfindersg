Freelancing: the first questions

Employee or self-employed? Who pays you, when, and what records to keep from day one.

What this coversOrientation from MOM and IRAS. Employment status depends on the actual relationship, and CPF or tax details may apply differently. Ask the official sources for your case.
Do this first · 2 to 10 minutes

Before you accept your next paid gig, write down the rate, the scope and the payment date and send it to the client.

Freelancing and gig work blur the line between employee and self-employed. Getting that right matters because it changes your rights and your tax.

Employee or self-employed?

MOM says whether you are an employee or a self-employed person ‘depends on the actual relationship’ between you and the company, and links a self-assessment tool. It says employees have a contract of service with benefits such as sick and annual leave and CPF contributions, while self-employed people have a contract for service and are not covered by the Employment Act or the Work Injury Compensation Act. [1]

Four things to settle before you start

  1. Scope: what exactly you are delivering and what counts as finished.
  2. Price: the rate, whether it is per hour, per piece or a fixed fee.
  3. Payment: how, when and what happens if it is late. Put it in writing.
  4. Records: keep invoices, receipts and payment proof from day one.

Tax

IRAS’s new-taxpayer page lists self-employment net profits above a set amount as one reason you may need to file even without being notified. IRAS also publishes a checklist for self-employed individuals on keeping accounts and filing. Check both for the current numbers. [2][3]

ImportantIf a ‘freelance job’ asks you to pay a deposit, receive money for someone else, or hand over your Singpass, stop. See Scam Shield.
What varies, so check yoursCPF, insurance and tax details for self-employed people. Confirm each on the official site.
