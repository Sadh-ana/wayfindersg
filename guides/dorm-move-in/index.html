Moving into a hall or dorm

A packing list sorted into bring, borrow and wait, so you do not arrive with three suitcases and no charger.

What this coversGeneral orientation. Rules differ by institution and even by residence block, so your hall’s own list always wins. No official source is claimed here.
Do this first · 2 to 10 minutes

Open your hall’s own move-in notice and highlight two items you must not forget and one you are not allowed to bring.

Move-in day goes better when you decide what has to be in the first bag, and let everything else arrive later. The first night needs a bed you can sleep in, a way to charge your phone, your documents and your regular medicine. Most of the rest can wait until you have seen the room.

Before you pack anything

  1. Find your hall’s own checklist or housing guide and read the prohibited-items section first. Appliances, cords, cooking gear and wall fixings are the usual restrictions.
  2. Check what the room already has: mattress size, desk, wardrobe, fan or air-conditioning, window type.
  3. Ask a senior or hall-mate what they wish they had brought. One real answer beats ten blog lists.

What can waitWall decor, extra storage boxes, desk lamps, a rice cooker or kettle, a bulk grocery run, a second set of everything. You will know what you need after three nights in the room, and most of these are a short trip away.
What varies, so check yoursBedding size, whether bedding is supplied, laundry payment method, cooking rules, visitor rules and move-in dates. Treat anything on this page as a prompt to check, not a ruling.
MythBring everything, you will need it.
Reality: Rooms are small. The usual regret is too much stuff and too few documents. Pack the essentials, then add things once you know the room.

Sources

This guide makes no official-rule claims, so it cites none. It is general orientation. Where rules matter (your residence, your bank, your employer), their own pages are the source.
