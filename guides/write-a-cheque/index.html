Write and deposit a cheque

How to fill one in without errors, what ‘crossed’ means, and why you may still be handed one in 2027.

What this coversBased on one bank’s instructions (POSB) and one commercial guide. Other banks may differ slightly; your cheque book and bank’s guidance prevail. We do not give clearing times because they vary by bank and are being modernised.
Do this first · 2 to 10 minutes

Practise with the cheque lab below, then check your bank’s own guide for its exact wording.

Cheques are fading, but you can still be handed one by an institution, landlord or tutor. Knowing how to write and deposit one is a ten-minute skill that saves a lot of confusion.

Write one, step by step

  1. Date: the date you write it. A future date makes it post-dated.
  2. Payee: the full name as in the recipient’s bank records.
  3. Amount in words, ending with ‘only’, then the amount in figures. The two must match.
  4. Cross it if you want it paid only to the named account: cross out ‘or bearer’ and draw two parallel lines at the top left.
  5. Sign the way your signature appears in your bank’s records.
  6. If you make a mistake, cancel it neatly with a horizontal line and sign next to the change, or write a fresh cheque. Use blue or black non-erasable ink.

    Depositing one, and what to know

    • POSB says a cheque is valid for six months from the date on it unless otherwise stated.
    • A post-dated cheque is deposited on or after the date written on it.
    • Corporate SGD cheque books stopped from 1 January 2026, and POSB says corporate cheques must be deposited by 11am on 31 December 2026. That tells you cheques are being phased down; personal cheques may follow, so ask your bank.

    [1][2]

    What replaced them

    For most payments, bank transfer and PayNow have taken over. If someone asks you to pay by cheque, check whether they accept PayNow or a bank transfer instead, which also leaves a clearer record.

    What varies, so check yoursClearing times, which branches or boxes accept deposits, and how long personal cheque books will continue. Ask your bank.
